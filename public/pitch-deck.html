The Problem

African Commerce is
Fragmented & Broken

📱
No Africa-first Commerce Platform

Instagram, TikTok, and Shopify exist but none are built for naira-based transactions, Nigerian logistics, or the local creator culture. African sellers are underserved with poor UX, high fees, and no live selling tools.

💸
Creators Can't Monetize Locally

Nigerian creators on TikTok earn USD but can't withdraw easily. Local platforms have no gifting economy, no subscription tools, no way to earn from live streams. The creator economy is essentially inaccessible for most Africans.

📦
Marketplace Trust Crisis

Jiji, OLX, WhatsApp commerce — all suffer from scam listings, no buyer protection, no escrow, no verified sellers. Over ₦2.7 trillion in e-commerce transactions are lost annually to fraud and mistrust.

🎯
No Live Auction Platform for Africa

Zero major African platforms offer real-time live auctions. The thrill economy (bidding wars, flash drops, limited editions) is completely untapped — a billion-dollar gap that Soko is purpose-built to fill.

Business Model

6 Revenue Streams.
All Active Day 1.

🛒
Marketplace Commission
5–15%

Per-category commission on every sale. Escrow held until delivery confirmed. Configurable per category.

🎁
Gift Revenue Split
30%

Soko earns 30% of all virtual gift transactions. Creators earn 70%. In-app coins must be purchased with real money first.

⭐
Subscription Revenue
10–20%

Platform cut from creator subscription tiers (₦500–₦5,000/mo). Creator keeps 80–90%. Growing recurring revenue base.

⚡
Ads & Boost
CPC/CPM

Sellers pay to boost listings, appear in search, get featured in Live discovery. Flash sale promotions. Banner placements.

🔨
Auction Fees
3–8%

Listing fee per auction + final-value fee on winning bid. Premium auction scheduling for guaranteed prime-time slots.

🪙
Coin Float Income
Float

Coins bought but not yet redeemed sit in Soko's escrow earning interest. Coin expiry policy creates breakage revenue.

Competitive Advantage

Why Soko Wins.
5 Defensible Moats.

🌍
Africa-First Architecture

Built from day 1 for Nigerian naira, African logistics, poor network conditions (3G-optimized streaming), and local gifting culture. Global competitors cannot retrofit this.

🔗
Network Effects

More creators → more buyers → more sellers → more gifting. A two-sided marketplace with a third creator layer creates compounding network effects that are nearly impossible to replicate once established.

🪙
Coin Economy Lock-In

Users who buy Soko Coins are committed to the platform. Coin balances, league rankings, and gift history create massive switching costs. This is the same mechanism that keeps billions on WeChat and TikTok.

📺
Live Commerce is Habit-Forming

Live commerce converts 10x better than static product pages (data from Taobao Live, TikTok Shop). Once users buy from a live stream, they return. This engagement pattern drives daily active use far beyond typical e-commerce.

🛡️
Data Moat

Every transaction, stream, bid, gift, and click trains Soko's recommendation and fraud models. By Year 2 we have behavioral data on millions of African consumers — a dataset no foreign competitor can buy or replicate.
